# 07-b Wedding planner assignment and fallback

## Delivered boundary

Either partner can invite one specific planner organization to a wedding with a deny-by-default, per-area `view`, `edit` and `download_export` ceiling. Invitations expire exactly 14 days after issuance. A UUID request key is bound to the normalized organization, ceiling and explicit reissue target: exact retries replay without duplicate audit/outbox facts, changed payloads conflict, and replacing a pending invitation is explicit. Partners can list and revoke their wedding's pending invitations. Organization owners and managers can list invitations addressed to their organization and accept or reject them. Cross-wedding, cross-organization and wrong-role operations fail without disclosing tenant data.

Acceptance atomically creates the active organization-to-wedding relationship, marks the invitation accepted and writes audit/outbox facts. A partial unique database invariant and serialized application checks enforce at most one active planner organization per wedding. Organization owners and managers may assign only active organization staff. Staff permissions are explicit and must be a subset of the couple ceiling; the organization can narrow access but cannot widen it. Assigning the same staff member again updates and reactivates the existing assignment, while explicit removal revokes that canonical assignment. Changing the lead clears the prior lead in the same transaction. A planner wedding-membership row is an immutable identity marker only: every authorization and workspace projection joins the current assignment, organization access, organization membership and couple restriction, and derives its permission overrides and scope version from those authoritative rows.

At most one active staff assignment is the optional lead. The fallback eligibility resolver accepts a one-use central authorization capability for the authenticated current actor and the exact requested wedding area and action; it never accepts or returns an actor identifier. It first resolves the wedding's designated lead and that lead's complete eligibility. If a non-absent eligible lead exists, only that lead is eligible. Manager fallback is considered only when no eligible lead exists, and then only for a distinct active assigned organization owner or manager who grants the exact operation and is not blocked, absent or revoked. An absent manager who is also the designated lead therefore cannot bypass themselves. A partner restriction immediately revokes the matching canonical assignment; lifting the block permits a later explicit organization reassignment but does not silently restore access. Revoking organization access immediately revokes all assignments and increments permission-scope versions, so cached authorization facts stop authorizing them. Deactivating organization staff locks the organization membership in the same access-then-membership order used by assignment and performs the same assignment revocation across every wedding in that organization, so a racing assignment cannot survive deactivation.

Typed `/api/v1` contracts and OpenAPI describe partner invitation/list/revoke, organization accept/reject, privacy-minimized active-access projections, staff assignment/update/removal, couple restriction, lead absence and access revocation routes. The wedding settings panel exposes localized invitation ceilings, assigned staff, block/lift-block and organization revocation controls. The organization screen exposes localized assignment, permission narrowing, lead transfer and removal controls. Every route returns explicit response DTOs rather than database rows or internal token, fingerprint, account or scope-version fields. Milestone 16 delivery and 07-c private notes/handover are deliberately absent.

Every couple-only planner-access mutation revalidates the exact authenticated actor as an active, unexpired partner for the wedding inside the transaction. A planner, coordinator, helper or read-only collaborator cannot issue, list or revoke organization invitations, manage individual restrictions, or revoke organization access even when a generic `settings:edit` override exists. Denials are privacy-safe and write no audit or outbox facts.

The canonical current active lead may transfer only that lead designation to another already-active, eligible, unblocked assignee in the same organization access. A one-use capability and access-row lock bind the authenticated actor, organization, access and target; transactional revalidation prevents stale, revoked and cross-tenant transfers and preserves the single-lead invariant. This narrow authority does not allow staff assignment, permission changes, removal, organization settings or couple controls. The localized lead-only management surface exposes just eligible-target transfer and the lead's own absence controls; organization-staff navigation and manager assignment/removal controls remain hidden, while server authorization remains authoritative.

## Owner-approved controlled policy

The frozen source leaves several workflow choices open. The owner explicitly approved this task policy before implementation:

1. One active planner organization is allowed per wedding.
2. Either partner may invite a specific organization.
3. Invitations expire after 14 days; active organization owners and managers may accept or reject.
4. Partners may revoke or explicitly reissue pending invitations with stable idempotency.
5. Planner access begins deny-by-default. The couple explicitly grants per-area View, Edit and Download ceilings; the organization may narrow but never widen them.
6. There is at most one optional lead planner. When absent, fallback is limited to active eligible staff within the couple ceiling.

This approval is recorded here, in the task brief and in the pull request. `docs/product/*` was not edited.

## Migration and runtime grants

Additive migration `0006_cynical_hannibal_king.sql` creates wedding organization invitations, active accesses, staff assignments and couple restrictions. Partial unique indexes enforce one pending invitation per wedding, one active organization per wedding and one active lead per access. Lifecycle, expiry and absence checks fail closed. Foreign keys are search-path relative so isolated disposable-schema verification resolves the intended schema.

The runtime manifest grants only exact table and column operations needed by the domain services. Runtime SQL can update only `last_opened_at` and `updated_at` on wedding memberships; it cannot update status, permission overrides or permission scope versions on either partner or planner rows. Planner membership authority is never persisted by a privileged mutation procedure: the immutable marker is inserted once and all lifecycle state remains in the narrowly granted planner assignment/access tables, eliminating a caller-forgeable cross-tenant security-definer boundary. Immutable tenant, actor, invitation, ceiling and issuance identity columns cannot be rewritten through runtime grants. The migration journal advances through the existing Drizzle migration runner; builds do not run migrations.

No Neon or persistent database was accessed. Do not run `pnpm test:database` against persistent databases. After owner merge, the separate migration agent may run the protected workflow against staging with the direct migration connection, then separately verify journal state and runtime grants. Production requires the workflow's additional production confirmation and separate authorization.

Rollback before persistent apply is reverting or closing the PR. After additive apply, stop using the routes and retain the tables; do not destructively drop wedding access history during incident response.
